The Educational Barrier

Practical experimentation is the cornerstone of STEM education. However, educational institutions in developing regions face severe financial strain in maintaining physical laboratories. Prohibitive costs for consumable chemical reagents, fragile glassware, and safety risks frequently reduce science instruction to passive textbook reading and static chalkboard demonstrations.

The AR Solution

V-Lab AR bypasses physical infrastructure entirely by utilizing standard consumer smartphones. By leveraging sensor fusion (RGB cameras + IMU sensors), it transforms any ordinary classroom desk into a fully equipped, interactive workstation—delivering a highly scalable, zero-risk, and cost-effective practical science platform.

"Under the Hood" Architecture

Engineering a high-fidelity AR experience on mobile devices requires rigorous spatial computing frameworks and strict asset optimization.

Marker-less Surface Detection

Instead of relying on printed QR codes, the system utilizes the RANSAC algorithm to filter 3D point clouds generated by the camera's feature extraction. It mathematically isolates stable, horizontal physical planes in real-time to generate renderable bounds for placing equipment.

Spatial Anchoring & VIO

To prevent virtual objects from drifting, assets are bound to Spatial Anchors rather than absolute camera coordinates. These anchors automatically recalculate their transformation matrices to absorb Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) loop-closure recalibrations, keeping virtual beakers rigidly locked to the physical desk.

3D Asset Optimization

To prevent mobile thermal throttling while running intensive SLAM calculations, assets follow strict optimization heuristics. Meshes undergo aggressive polygon decimation, and materials are combined into Texture Atlases to leverage Static and Dynamic Draw Call Batching in the Unity engine.

Real-time Mathematical Engine

The application replaces static pre-rendered animations with custom C# physics and chemistry logic. Calculations like $V=IR$ or stoichiometric molar equations run continuously in the background, updating UI gauges and grading student inputs in real-time to enforce a genuine cause-and-effect loop.
